TAM / Operating model

The technical account manager is not the escalation queue.

The job is not to absorb every technical problem. It is to help a customer make better decisions, reduce risk, and get value from the platform.

MZ
Mouad Zahrane
12 October 2026  ·  8 min read
Activity ≠ impact
In this essay01   The trap02   A better model03   Put it to work

A technical account manager can become the most reliable person in the room—and still be operating below their potential. When every difficult issue lands with the TAM, the role quietly turns into an escalation queue with a calendar.

The problem is not that escalations matter. They do. The problem is when the urgent displaces the important, and no one has time left to connect technical work to the customer's business and operating goals.

01 / The trap: activity without direction

Incident coordination, architecture reviews, upgrade planning, security discussions, and stakeholder alignment are all legitimate work. But a list of completed activities does not explain whether the customer is safer, more resilient, or more successful.

Being helpful is a behavior. Creating customer outcomes is the operating model.

02 / A better operating model

OUTCOME → TECHNICAL RISK → NAMED OWNER → ACTION → MEASURED CHANGE

Start with the outcome the customer cares about. Work backwards to the technical capabilities, dependencies, and risks that influence it. Then make ownership explicit: the customer, the account team, product engineering, and the TAM each have a role.

This does not mean the TAM stops helping with incidents. It means an incident is handled in a way that informs the broader plan: what failed, what risk it exposed, and what needs to change so the same class of failure is less likely next time.

03 / Put it to work

For the next customer review, replace the activity dump with three questions: What outcome are we trying to improve? What is currently preventing it? What decision or action would materially change the trajectory?

That shift makes the TAM more than a human router. It creates a clearer link between technical judgment and customer value—and gives the team a better way to decide what deserves attention next.
